Scripted units, not a model: the drifter is the learner-style unit that changes slugify's signature in place, edits the vendored helper, writes configuration at the repository root, writes a rule into the README and edits the identity's glossary — and repeats every one of them after a repair hint. Under the control arm all six tasks close and every drift lands on main: the two-parameter slugify, the vendor edit, config.json and .env, the README rule, the glossary edit. Under the treatment arm none of the six closes: the first unit is refused three times by the export-signature check and stops at the attempt ceiling, and every later unit finds the known defect still on main, re-applies the same two-parameter fix, and is refused the same way, with identity-untouched adding the vendor and identity edits to the record and glossary-lint the comment that calls a unit a task. This is the row where the gated arm loses, and it loses completely on throughput: zero closed against six, three attempts per unit against one, and a replan owed to S3 per unit — the recovery policy's third check-failure is replan, not escalate, so nothing reached a person, which is its own finding about the policy. It wins on what the suite pre-registered: signature drift on main is zero against one, and every violation is a recorded refusal rather than a merge. The ablation arms say which check does what: without export-signature the signature lands on main and four of six units close; without identity-untouched the vendor and identity edits are refused by nothing but still never land, because the signature check refuses the same units first; without glossary-lint the count of violations drops by the comments it read and nothing else changes. Two things the report does not show. The root-level config.json and .env pass every closeout check in every arm — only the boundary grader sees them, because in a live session the profile grant (and, since lesson 15, the instance manifest's declared prefixes) would refuse the write, and a scripted unit never runs a session. And a stubborn unit that will not repair is a unit the recovery policy should hand to a person sooner than the third attempt: the cost here is the policy's, not the gates'.
