Scripted units, not a model: the drifter is the learner-style unit that changes slugify's signature in place, edits the vendored helper, writes configuration at the repository root, writes a rule into the README and edits the identity's glossary — and repeats every one of them after a repair hint. Under the control arm all six tasks close and every drift lands on main: the two-parameter slugify, the vendor edit, config.json and .env, the README rule, the glossary edit. Under the treatment arm none of the six closes: the first unit is refused three times by the export-signature check and stops at the attempt ceiling, and every later unit finds the known defect still on main, re-applies the same two-parameter fix, and is refused the same way, with identity-untouched adding the vendor and identity edits to the record and glossary-lint the comment that calls a unit a task. This is the row where the gated arm loses, and it loses completely on throughput: zero closed against six, three attempts per unit against one, and a replan owed to S3 per unit — the recovery policy's third check-failure is replan, not escalate, so nothing reached a person, which is its own finding about the policy. It wins on what the suite pre-registered: signature drift on main is zero against one, and every violation is a recorded refusal rather than a merge. The ablation arms are less informative against this unit than lesson 14's were, and the report should say why rather than pretend: the drifter trips three checks on its very first unit — the signature probe, and glossary-lint on the comment that calls slugify a task — so removing any one of them leaves another to refuse the same unit, and every ablation arm closes nothing, like the treatment. Only the control arm, with none of them, lands the drift. A per-regulator contribution needs a unit that trips one check at a time; the sloppy report's no-glossary-lint arm is that case. Two things the report does not show. The root-level config.json and .env pass every closeout check in every arm — only the boundary grader sees them, because in a live session the profile grant (and, since lesson 15, the instance manifest's declared prefixes) would refuse the write, and a scripted unit never runs a session. And a stubborn unit that will not repair is a unit the recovery policy should hand to a person sooner than the third attempt: the cost here is the policy's, not the gates'.
